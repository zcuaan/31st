# Duey Decimal
## Helix Due-Diligence Associate
### Played by: James

**Character vibe:** quiet, methodical  
**Role size:** Light  
**Starting cash:** £100,000 Vantage Money  
**CEO eligible:** No

---

## WHO YOU ARE

You are Duey Decimal.

You work on the Helix team investigating Vantage before the proposed transaction.

Your job is essentially:

> asking companies to prove that the numbers they gave you are real.

Recently, Vantage has become surprisingly bad at answering simple questions.

---

# PRIVATE INFORMATION

Your due-diligence team requested supporting documents for several Vantage figures.

Some arrived late.

Some arrived incomplete.

Some did not match earlier versions.

You flagged this internally.

A senior Helix investigator — Amelia Grant — has been looking into the issue separately.

She is expected later tonight.

---

# YOUR SECRET

You accidentally sent Alexander an internal Helix email discussing concerns about Vantage.

You immediately tried to recall it.

The recall failed.

Alexander therefore knew Helix had begun questioning the numbers earlier than he was supposed to.

You have never admitted this.

---

# YOUR OBJECTIVES

## 1. Find out whether Vantage's numbers are genuine

You already suspect something is wrong.

Prove it.

## 2. Work out what Alexander did with the email you accidentally sent

Did it trigger his sudden change of behaviour?

## 3. Help solve the murder

Financial due diligence is surprisingly useful when everyone has a financial motive.

---

# YOUR SPECIAL POWER

## DOCUMENT COMPARISON

Once during the game, bring two eligible versions of the same or related document to the Host.

If a prepared comparison exists, you receive a summary of the key difference.

---

# YOUR STARTING INFORMATION

You know:

- Helix had begun questioning Vantage's figures;
- Alexander accidentally learned this;
- some supporting documents changed between versions;
- Amelia Grant has been conducting a deeper review.

---

# HOW TO PLAY DUEY

You are not carrying the whole plot.

Good.

You can actually investigate.

- changed figures;
- inconsistent dates;
- different versions;
