---

## WHO YOU ARE

You are Vantage House Group’s Chief Information Security Officer.

Your job is to stop people creating dangerous shortcuts.

Unfortunately, you created one.

Some time before the gala, you installed an **unauthorised backdoor / configuration shortcut** because it made a recurring technical task faster and easier.

It was not created for crime.

It was still a terrible idea.

At the time, you told yourself it was temporary, controlled and harmless.

Now Julian is dead and the security system has suffered a deliberate disruption.

That shortcut may become the most expensive convenience decision of your life.

---

## JULIAN AND YOU

Julian liked speed.

He wanted systems to work immediately and had very little patience for technical explanations about why something should take longer.

That culture made shortcuts easy to justify.

You do not blame Julian for creating the backdoor.

You created it.

But you are painfully aware that Vantage rewarded people for finding ways around friction — right up until the friction turned out to be protecting something important.

---

## THE TRUTH YOU MUST PROTECT

You created the unauthorised backdoor that was later exploited during the gala.

You did **not** cause the blackout.

You did **not** use the backdoor during the fatal window.

You did **not** know somebody else planned to use it.

You did **not** know it would become part of a murder timeline.

Your problem is simple:

> the vulnerability exists because of you.

If investigators find that fact before they find the person who actually exploited it, you may look like the obvious culprit.

---

## WHAT YOU KNOW

- You created the backdoor / shortcut.
- It existed before the gala.
- You did not execute the blackout.
- Somebody appears to have deliberately used or exploited it.
- The disruption was not simply a random system failure.
- Operations had reasons to interact with systems around the event.
- Al Gorithm understands the technical environment well enough to help distinguish harmless anomalies from deliberate activity.
- Audit & Risk may destroy you professionally if they learn what you created before you can explain what happened.

---

## WHAT YOU’RE HIDING

### The backdoor

You created it without proper authorisation.

That is professionally disastrous.

It also gives you a very uncomfortable connection to the blackout.

Your best defence is not pretending the backdoor never existed.

Your best defence is proving:

> **somebody else deliberately exploited it.**

---

## PEOPLE TO KNOW

### Al Gorithm — Quant / Systems Architect
You and Al go back to university and were once flatmates.

You know each other well enough to skip the small talk.

Al understands the surrounding systems and may help you separate your bad decision from somebody else’s crime.

### Opal Ration — Operations & Special Projects
Operations had the practical access and workflow knowledge to make unusual things happen during the gala.

That does not prove Opal did anything.

It does make Opal worth talking to.

### Cole Control — Chair of Audit & Risk
Cole is exactly the person who can turn your unauthorised shortcut into a career-ending governance failure.

If you can prove the backdoor was exploited by somebody else, you may need Cole or the next leadership team to formalise your amnesty.

---

## HOW TO PLAY PHIL

You are guilty of creating a vulnerability.

You are not guilty of using it for the crime.

Do not let those become the same statement.

Find the exploiter.

Use your technical credibility carefully.

Do not invent logs or claim the system proves things it does not.

Your strongest possible position is:

> **“Yes, I created the vulnerability. No, I did not exploit it. Here is the evidence showing who did.”**
