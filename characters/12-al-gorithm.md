# AL GORITHM
## Quant / Systems Architect

**Vibe:** analytical • curious • slightly chaotic  
**Starting cash:** £150,000  
**CEO eligible:** NO

---

## QUICK START

### YOUR OBJECTIVES

1. **Clear your own unauthorised system/model change.**
2. **Identify which system event actually matters to Julian’s timeline.**

### YOUR POWER — SYSTEMS ANALYSIS

**Once per game**, choose one currently available item marked `SYSTEM`.

Give your ability card to the GM to receive one prepared **systems analysis** result for free.

You may keep, trade, sell or reveal the result.

Your power cannot unlock evidence that is not yet in play.

---

## WHO YOU ARE

You are a quant and systems architect.

You like systems because, unlike people, they usually admit when two things cannot both be true.

Unfortunately, Vantage’s systems are full of human decisions.

You recently ran an **unauthorised system/model experiment**.

It was not criminal.

It was not connected to Julian’s murder.

It was still exactly the kind of thing that looks terrible once investigators start asking why the system behaved strangely.

---

## JULIAN AND YOU

Julian liked clever people who could make complicated things useful.

He had less patience for technical caution.

You often admired his instinct and hated his habit of assuming that somebody could tidy the system up afterwards.

You were not part of his inner political circle.

You were one of the people expected to make the infrastructure keep up.

---

## THE TRUTH YOU MUST PROTECT

You made a system/model change without proper approval.

It created or contributed to an anomaly that may appear in logs or technical review.

That anomaly is **not the blackout**.

It is **not the fatal event**.

Your problem is proving that before everybody decides the strange system trace must belong to the murderer.

---

## WHAT YOU KNOW

- Your own change exists and may look suspicious.
- It is unrelated to Julian’s death.
- Phil McCracken understands Vantage security and goes back with you to university.
- The gala blackout appears to involve a different kind of system activity.
- Operations interacts with systems in ways that pure technical logs do not always explain.
- If investigators compare timestamps carefully, some system events may help establish whether Julian was still active later than people assumed.

---

## WHAT YOU’RE HIDING

You bypassed proper approval for your experiment.

You did it because the test was useful and you thought the risk was small.

You do not want people treating your bad process decision as proof you caused the blackout or Julian’s death.

Your goal is to separate:

> **harmless-but-unauthorised anomaly**

from

> **system event that actually matters to the timeline.**

---

## PEOPLE TO KNOW

### Phil McCracken — Chief Information Security Officer
You and Phil were university friends and former flatmates.

You know each other well enough to skip the small talk.

Phil understands the security layer.

You understand the broader system behaviour.

### Vera Iance — Risk Modelling
Vera understands assumptions, models and when apparently neat numbers are hiding ugly reality.

You often speak the same technical language from different angles.

### Opal Ration — Operations & Special Projects
Opal understands practical workflows and access.

If a system event was caused by somebody doing something operationally unusual, Opal may know what that would look like.

---

## HOW TO PLAY AL

Do not pretend every log line is meaningful.

Your value is being able to say:

> **“This looks strange, but it is not the strange thing that matters.”**

Find the important timestamp.

Find the event that changes the murder timeline.

Clear your own change without hiding the fact that you made it.

You are allowed to be technically guilty of bad process and still useful to the investigation.
