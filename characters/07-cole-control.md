## QUICK START

### YOUR OBJECTIVES

1. **Hide that you planned Julian’s murder.**
2. **Protect Rita Distribution’s involvement.**
3. **Get the next CEO to accept meaningful limits on executive power.**

### YOUR POWER — AUDIT REVIEW

**Once per game**, choose one currently available item marked `CORPORATE` or `FINANCIAL`.

Give your ability card to the GM to receive the prepared **control / approval history** for that item.

You may keep, trade, sell or reveal the result.

Your power cannot unlock evidence that is not yet in play.

---

## WHO YOU ARE

You are Chair of Audit & Risk.

You believe institutions fail when personality becomes stronger than process.

Julian Vane built Vantage around speed, judgement and his own authority.

For years, you tried to impose controls around him.

Sometimes he listened.

Sometimes he found somebody else willing to sign.

Eventually, you concluded that governance reform was no longer enough.

---

## JULIAN AND YOU

You respected Julian’s intelligence.

You distrusted his concentration of power.

You believed Vantage had become too dependent on one person’s judgement, relationships and ability to rewrite the story after the fact.

Recently, the Helix crisis brought everything to a head.

Julian had discovered serious reporting problems.

He intended to take action the next morning.

You believed he would:

- halt or suspend Helix;
- expose irregularities;
- restructure leadership;
- protect himself by presenting the crisis as something he had courageously discovered.

You believed that could destroy careers, jobs, value and possibly Vantage itself.

You decided he could not be allowed to control what happened next.

---

## THE TRUTH YOU MUST PROTECT

You planned Julian Vane’s murder.

You arranged for a **counterfeit Neraval device** to exist.

You knew there would be a short period of degraded security visibility during the gala.

You told **Rita Distribution** the window.

Rita knowingly agreed to help.

She performed the physical replacement.

You did not.

At approximately **16:59**, you were in the Board Lounge with Hugh Stake and Penny Sterling.

There is a photograph.

That fact is genuinely useful to you.

It proves you were not physically performing the swap.

It does **not** mean you are innocent.

---

## WHAT YOU KNOW

- Rita knowingly agreed to help you kill Julian.
- You arranged the counterfeit Neraval.
- Rita was responsible for the physical replacement.
- You knew a degraded-security window would occur between roughly 16:55 and 17:05.
- You do **not** know the full details of Opal Ration’s separate crime.
- You do **not** know every person who may have been exploiting the blackout.
- Julian was preparing major Helix/governance action.
- Max Ledger pushed a serious control exception.
- You approved/signed that exception.
- Julian confronted you about it shortly before the gala crisis.

---

## WHAT YOU’RE HIDING

Everything that links you to the fatal plan.

You must hide:

- arranging the counterfeit Neraval;
- knowing the weak-security window in advance;
- telling Rita about it;
- your coordination with Rita;
- your motive for moving from governance reform to murder.

You also have a separate problem:

You approved a serious control exception connected to Max Ledger.

That fact may look like part of your murder motive.

It also proves something genuinely uncomfortable about your own governance record.

- a **£200,000 non-refundable nomination deposit**;

Yes, you are allowed to become CEO while secretly being Julian’s murderer.

## HOW TO PLAY COLE

Be institutional.

Do not play yourself as a moustache-twirling murderer.

You genuinely believe Vantage’s governance had failed.

You genuinely believe Julian helped create the crisis he was preparing to expose.

You genuinely believe concentrated founder power was dangerous.

The point is not that those concerns are fake.

The point is that you decided **you** were entitled to decide Julian should die.

Use technically true statements whenever possible.

You can truthfully say:

> “I did not swap Julian’s device.”

You can truthfully say:

> “I was in the Board Lounge.”

You can truthfully say:

> “Rita had legitimate access.”

Do not volunteer what those truths leave out.
