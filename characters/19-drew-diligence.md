# DREW DILIGENCE
## Helix Due-Diligence Associate

**Vibe:** conscientious • nervous • detail-focused  
**Starting cash:** £100,000  
**CEO eligible:** NO

---

## QUICK START

### YOUR OBJECTIVES

1. **Prove that the Helix submissions changed materially.**
2. **Get your document comparison into the hands of somebody who can independently validate it.**

### YOUR POWER — DOCUMENT COMPARISON

**Once per game**, choose two currently available related document versions.

Give your ability card to the GM to receive the prepared **document comparison**.

The result identifies meaningful differences between the two versions.

You may keep, trade, sell or reveal it.

Your power cannot unlock evidence that is not yet in play.

---

## WHO YOU ARE

You are a due-diligence associate working around the Helix transaction.

You live in the world of:

- version numbers;
- appendices;
- revised assumptions;
- tracked changes;
- documents that are supposedly “basically the same.”

You recently discovered that two Helix submissions were **not basically the same**.

The changes were important.

You are worried that somebody will ask why you saw the difference and did not escalate it faster.

---

## JULIAN AND YOU

You did not work closely with Julian personally.

You knew that Helix mattered enormously to him.

You also knew senior people wanted the transaction to keep moving.

That made every awkward question feel expensive.

Recently, you came across conflicting versions of Helix material.

At first, you thought you had received the wrong attachment or an outdated draft.

Then you started comparing them.

The differences did not look accidental.

---

## THE TRUTH YOU MUST PROTECT

You discovered conflicting Helix versions before Julian died.

You did not immediately turn the whole company upside down.

You were unsure whether:

- one was simply obsolete;
- you had been copied accidentally;
- the changes had been properly approved somewhere else;
- or you were looking at something much worse.

You are now worried that people will interpret your hesitation as complicity.

You need to prove what actually changed.

## WHAT YOU’RE HIDING

You saw the conflicting materials earlier than you would now like to admit.

You hesitated.

That does not mean you changed them.

It does mean you may have had information before senior people expected you to.

Your strongest defence is to prove:

> **the documents changed — and somebody else needs to explain why.**

---

## HOW TO PLAY DREW

Compare things.

Do not let people hand-wave a document difference away as “just another version.”

Ask:

- what changed?
- when?
- who knew?
- does the change affect the conclusion?
- is there an independent source that confirms which version is closer to reality?

You do not need to be the loudest investigator.

You need to make one comparison impossible to ignore.
