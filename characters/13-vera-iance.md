# VERA IANCE
## Risk Modelling

**Vibe:** precise • sceptical • quietly vindicated  
**Starting cash:** £200,000  
**CEO eligible:** NO

---

## QUICK START

### YOUR OBJECTIVES

1. **Prove that your warnings about Vantage’s aggressive assumptions were justified.**
2. **Get the next CEO to commit to independent risk sign-off.**

### YOUR POWER — RISK MODEL

**Once per game**, choose one currently available financial, modelling or risk item.

Give your ability card to the GM to receive the prepared **assumption / sensitivity analysis** for that item.

You may keep, trade, sell or reveal the result.

Your power cannot unlock evidence that is not yet in play.

---

## WHO YOU ARE

You work in Risk Modelling.

Your job is to ask what happens if the optimistic assumptions are wrong.

This makes you useful when things go badly and unpopular when things are going well.

You warned that some Vantage and Helix assumptions were becoming too aggressive.

You were ignored.

Now Julian is dead, Helix is unstable and people are suddenly very interested in who raised concerns before the crisis.

You would prefer not to say “I told you so.”

You may say it anyway.

---

## JULIAN AND YOU

Julian did not hate risk.

He hated risk that prevented action.

You believed Vantage had started treating optimistic scenarios as if they were neutral ones.

Julian sometimes listened to you.

Other times he chose growth, speed and narrative confidence instead.

You did not want him dead.

You wanted the company to stop pretending risk disappeared because senior people found it inconvenient.

---

## WHAT YOU KNOW

- Some Helix assumptions were too aggressive.
- You raised concerns.
- Max Ledger pushed for interpretations that kept the growth story moving.
- Audit/source evidence may support what you were seeing.
- Al Gorithm understands the technical/model side of unusual system behaviour.
- The next CEO will have an opportunity to either strengthen independent risk oversight or repeat the same culture.

---

## WHAT YOU’RE HIDING

Nothing murder-critical.

Your personal risk is reputational:

If your warnings were too vague, too late or badly documented, senior people may claim you are rewriting history now that something has gone wrong.

You need evidence.

You do not just want people to believe you.

You want the record to show you were right.

---

## PEOPLE TO KNOW

### Al Gorithm — Quant / Systems Architect
Al understands models and systems from a different angle.

You may be able to help each other separate genuine signal from technical noise.

### Max Ledger — Group Strategy Director
Max pushed the aggressive assumptions you disliked.

You respect his intelligence.

You do not trust his definition of acceptable risk.

### Audie Trail — Internal Audit
Audie may have source records that show whether the numbers and assumptions reaching decision-makers matched the underlying evidence.

That could validate your warnings.

---

## HOW TO PLAY VERA

Be specific.

Do not say:

> “The numbers were bad.”

Say:

> “Which assumption changed?”

> “Who approved it?”

> “What happens if we use the less optimistic case?”

Your murder role is indirect.

Financial evidence creates motive, pressure and red herrings.

You are one of the people who can help the room understand **why Julian was preparing to act** without pretending that financial wrongdoing automatically proves murder.
