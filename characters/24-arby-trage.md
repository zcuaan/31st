# ARBY TRAGE
## Special Situations Investor / Restructuring Adviser

**Vibe:** discreet • predatory • extremely polite  
**Starting cash:** £650,000  
**CEO eligible:** NO

---

## QUICK START

### YOUR OBJECTIVES

1. **Finish at least £300,000 richer than you started.**
2. **Broker two meaningful signed deals involving money, information or succession support.**

### YOUR POWER — OFF-MARKET OFFER

**Once per game**, write an offer containing:

- the target;
- the amount you are offering;
- what you want in return.

Give it to the GM.

The GM privately delivers it **anonymously**.

The target may **accept or reject**.

That is the whole process.

There is no automatic counter-offer round.

---

## WHO YOU ARE

You invest in awkward situations.

Distressed companies.

Emergency financings.

Restructurings.

Assets somebody suddenly needs to sell.

Problems that become opportunities once everybody else is uncomfortable enough to negotiate.

You are not here to solve everybody’s problems.

You are here to work out:

> **what everybody’s problems are worth.**

---

## JULIAN AND YOU

Julian understood leverage.

So do you.

You respected his ability to build Vantage into something large enough that one crisis could create opportunities in every direction.

You had no reason to want him dead.

His death has simply made:

- liquidity;
- control;
- information;
- votes;
- distressed assets;

more valuable than they were an hour ago.

## THE TRUTH YOU MUST PROTECT

Before the gala, you were quietly approached about possible **emergency liquidity / restructuring against assets connected to Fran de Fonds’ family office**.

You do not necessarily know the entire problem yet.

You know enough to understand that somebody may be under pressure.

That gives you a useful reason to speak to:

- Fran;
- Cal Lateral;
- anyone who might fund, guarantee or restructure the situation.

Do not give the solution away cheaply.

## HOW TO PLAY ARBY

Ask people what they need.

Then ask what they can offer.

Do not rescue people for free.

A good Arby conversation sounds like:

> “Interesting problem.”

> “How urgent is it?”

> “Who else knows?”

> “What would you give me if I solved it tonight?”

You can make money without touching the murder.

You can also make money **because** of the murder, if information or succession support becomes valuable.
