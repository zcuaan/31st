# GM FILE — MAX LEDGER
## Group Strategy Director, Vantage House Group

**Player-facing file:** `characters/01-max-ledger.md`  
**Core function:** major financial red herring + succession protagonist  
**Murder involvement:** NONE

---

## CORE TRUTH

Max is deliberately one of the strongest suspects in the game.

He is:

- ambitious
- financially compromised
- genuinely culpable in Vantage’s reporting culture
- personally exposed by Helix
- a serious Interim CEO contender

But he is **not involved in Julian Vane’s murder**.

Max did **not**:

- plan Julian’s death
- assist Cole or Rita
- source or handle the counterfeit Neraval
- swap Julian’s device
- put Aster-9 in Julian’s champagne
- create the blackout
- enter Julian’s office during the fatal sequence
- steal Julian’s USB
- disturb the crime scene

The intended distinction is:

> **Max is guilty of serious financial/corporate wrongdoing. He is innocent of Julian’s murder.**

Do not soften the financial wrongdoing simply because he is innocent of homicide.

---

## EXACT FINANCIAL WRONGDOING

Max:

- pushed aggressive valuation and reporting decisions
- pressured colleagues to make assumptions and interpretations work in Vantage’s favour
- pushed for a major control exception when governance slowed growth
- knew Helix was not receiving a completely clean picture
- still wanted Helix to proceed
- benefited personally from continued growth, valuation and succession prospects
- knew other people were carrying risks that ultimately benefited him
- had started trying to discover what evidence Julian had collected and who had seen it

His internal rationalisation:

- the underlying business is real
- the discrepancies are manageable
- risk functions cannot veto growth forever
- problems can be corrected later
- senior executives are paid to make difficult decisions

He is not surprised that something is wrong.

He is surprised by how far it has gone and by Julian’s decision to stop/suspend Helix.

---

## THE CONTROL EXCEPTION

Max requested/pushed a controversial control exception.

**Cole Control approved it.**

The original signed document begins in **Cole’s possession**.

Max knows:

- the exception exists
- he pushed for it
- Cole signed/approved it

Max does **not** begin holding the signed document.

### Why it matters

For Max:
- it supports the case that he pressured governance to weaken controls

For Cole:
- it shows he signed the exception
- it helps establish a serious governance conflict with Julian
- it does **not** by itself prove murder

An archived version later becomes available so the issue cannot be permanently suppressed.

## COMMON GM QUESTIONS

### “Was Max really on a phone call?”
If the relevant result has been obtained:

> Yes. A genuine business call occurred during the recorded period.

### “Does the call prove Max could not have killed Julian?”
> The call is genuine, but it does not account for every second of Max’s physical movements.

### “Did Max request the control exception?”
If evidence obtained:

> Yes.

### “Did Cole approve it?”
If evidence obtained:

> Yes.

Do not imply that this alone proves fraud or murder.

### “What did Julian mean by ‘You signed it’?”
> Max heard those words. He did not hear enough of the conversation to know exactly what Julian meant.

### “Did Max know the numbers were false?”
Use the nuance:

> Max knew some reporting and assumptions were becoming increasingly difficult to defend and still pushed for Helix to proceed.

Do not reduce this to either:
- Max knew nothing, or
- Max personally fabricated every number.

### “Did Max know about the counterfeit Neraval?”
> There is no established evidence that Max knew about or handled the counterfeit Neraval.

Avoid introducing that term before the relevant evidence family is public.
