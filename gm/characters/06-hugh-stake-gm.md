# GM FILE — HUGH STAKE
## Cornerstone Investor

**Player-facing file:** `characters/06-hugh-stake.md`  
**Core function:** kingmaker / high-liquidity information buyer / Cole alibi witness  
**Murder involvement:** NONE

## CORE TRUTH

Hugh is not involved in Julian Vane’s murder.

He is one of the most powerful succession players because he controls **3 of 9 weighted votes**, and all three votes are indivisible.

Hugh is designed to:
- create a market for candidate information;
- force CEO candidates to make concrete promises;
- give Olivier a powerful but readable role;
- provide a genuine witness to Cole’s physical location during Rita’s swap;
- demonstrate that a physical alibi for the swap does **not** clear Cole as mastermind.

## SUCCESSION POSITION

Hugh holds **3 indivisible votes**.

He is **not CEO-eligible**.

A candidate needs 5 votes to win.

Hugh can:
- accept bribes;
- demand written promises;
- sell his stated support;
- change his mind;
- betray a deal;
- vote for any eligible nominated candidate.

No private promise binds his ballot.

## CANONICAL TIMELINE — HUGH

### ~16:59
Hugh is in the Board Lounge with:
- Cole Control
- Penny Sterling

A photograph is taken.

This is a **genuine physical alibi for Cole for the actual Neraval swap**, which Rita performs at approximately 16:58.

Critical GM rule:
**Do not let players turn this into proof that Cole is innocent of the murder.**

Cole did not physically perform the swap.
Cole planned the murder and arranged for Rita to do it.

Hugh does not know that distinction at the start.

## LUCY LEAKS

Before the gala, Hugh privately retained **Lucy Leaks** to map succession factions and candidate vulnerabilities.

Hugh knows:
- he hired Lucy;
- the assignment concerns succession intelligence;
- he expects discreet, useful information.

Hugh does NOT know:
- Lee Verage also hired Lucy;
- Lucy is balancing two clients.

Hugh’s copy of the retainer note is a **portable private game item**.

It may be shown, traded, hidden or stolen if left unattended.

It is not murder-critical.

## WHAT HUGH GENUINELY KNOWS AT START

Hugh knows:
- Helix matters enormously to Vantage’s value and future;
- Max Ledger wants the CEO job;
- Lee Verage has serious views about ownership, governance and leadership;
- Cole Control presents himself as a governance/risk stabiliser;
- Opal Ration and Vince Ture are eligible succession candidates;
- Hugh’s own three votes are likely to make him the central kingmaker;
- he hired Lucy Leaks;
- he was with Cole and Penny shortly before Julian’s death;
- a photograph was taken during that Board Lounge conversation.

Hugh does not know the exact evidential timestamp of the photograph until it enters evidence.

## POWER — KINGMAKER DUE DILIGENCE

Once per game, **after nominations close**, Hugh chooses one nominated candidate.

The GM gives Hugh the prepared confidential candidate-risk memo for that candidate.

The memo:
- uses already-established canon;
- may include commercial, governance, reputation or exposure risks;
- does not reveal the murderer directly;
- cannot unlock evidence that is not yet in play;
- is private to Hugh unless he sells/reveals it.

The ability card itself is non-transferable.
The information obtained may be sold or traded.

Before party day, prepare one short risk memo for every candidate who may plausibly be nominated.

## OBJECTIVES — GM INTERPRETATION

### 1. Decide who becomes Interim CEO
Use his three votes strategically. He does not need to announce support early.

### 2. Obtain a concrete signed concession from the eventual winner
A vague promise such as “I’ll listen to shareholders” should not count.

Examples that count:
- a specific governance commitment;
- a board/strategy commitment;
- a signed commercial concession;
- a defined reporting or oversight commitment.

### 3. Learn meaningful risk information about at least two candidates
One may come from Kingmaker Due Diligence.

The second must come through investigation, evidence, deals, Lucy or ordinary conversation.

## INTENDED PLAY ARC

### Early game
Hugh should feel rich, calm and sought-after. Players should quickly understand that his votes matter.

### After death
He has a practical reason to investigate:
- the killer may become CEO;
- murder evidence changes candidate value;
- information about the murder is tradable leverage.

### Succession
Candidates should pitch him. Other players should sell him information. He should be able to demand signed promises without learning a new mechanic.

### Late murder game
The Board Lounge photograph may make Cole look less suspicious because Cole could not have performed the swap himself.

That is intentional.

The game should later distinguish:
**physical executor ≠ architect.**

## EVIDENCE CONNECTIONS

The Board Lounge photograph is canon but its final evidence ID is **TBD** until the master evidence registry is finalized.

Do not create a new evidence ID inside Hugh’s pack.

Hugh’s private Lucy retainer note is a starter prop, not official murder evidence.

## ABSENCE CONTINGENCY

Hugh is a spine role because he holds 3 votes.

If Olivier cannot attend:
- do not simply delete the votes;
- reassign the voting block or use a prepared proxy mechanism before the party;
- do not improvise this during the game.

His murder-clue function must also be preserved through the photograph itself and Penny’s knowledge.

## COMMON GM QUESTIONS

### “Can Hugh split his three votes?”
No. His three votes are indivisible.

### “Can Hugh take a bribe and vote differently?”
Yes.

### “Does a signed deal bind Hugh?”
No. It proves a promise existed; it does not enforce the ballot.

### “Does Hugh being with Cole prove Cole did not kill Julian?”
It supports that Cole did not personally perform the physical swap at that moment.
It does not establish whether Cole planned, arranged or assisted the fatal plot.

### “Does Hugh know the photo time?”
At the start, no exact evidential timestamp. He remembers the Board Lounge interaction and that a photograph was taken.

## FINAL QA

Hugh passes if:
- Olivier can understand the role in a few minutes;
- his three votes create immediate social gravity;
- he has clear reasons to talk to candidates and information sellers;
- he does not need to memorise murder mechanics;
- his power resolves in under 30 seconds;
- the Cole alibi clue is useful but not exculpatory;
- Lucy’s retainer gives him one private intrigue without creating a subplot tree.
