# GM FILE — COLE CONTROL
## Chair of Audit & Risk

**Player-facing file:** `characters/07-cole-control.md`  
**Core function:** mastermind / governance radical / CEO candidate  
**Murder involvement:** ARCHITECT / MASTERMIND

---

## CORE TRUTH

Cole Control planned Julian Vane’s murder.

He is the architect.

Rita Distribution is the knowing accomplice and physical swapper.

Cole:

- decided Julian had to die;
- arranged the counterfeit Neraval;
- knew a weak-security window would occur;
- told Rita the window;
- relied on Rita to perform the physical replacement;
- later benefits from a genuine physical alibi for the swap itself.

Cole did not physically swap the Neraval.

That distinction is deliberate.

The murder solution must establish:

> **physical executor ≠ architect.**

---

## MOTIVE

Cole is a governance institutionalist.

He believes Julian’s founder power became structurally dangerous.

He spent years trying to restrain Julian through process, controls and oversight.

The Helix crisis radicalised him.

Julian planned to:

- halt/suspend the transaction;
- expose manipulation/irregularities;
- restructure leadership;
- control the public story;
- potentially scapegoat people who had operated inside the aggressive culture Julian himself created.

Cole believed the result could destroy:

- jobs;
- careers;
- enterprise value;
- Vantage’s survival.

His core moral failure is not that he identified no real governance problem.

It is:

> he appointed himself judge of Julian’s right to live.

---

## EXACT CANONICAL TIMELINE — COLE

### Before gala
Cole has already decided Julian must die.

He arranges the counterfeit Neraval through a fictional traceable route.

He knows Rita will perform the swap.

He has noticed/learned that a weak-security window will occur.

Important:
Cole does **not** know the full Opal/Vince side-crime specifics.

He exploits the existence of the window, not the whole side plot.

### 16:30
Cole and Julian have a tense conversation near Julian’s office about:

- governance;
- Helix;
- responsibility;
- signed approvals / control exceptions.

Max Ledger catches the end.

Max clearly hears Julian say:

> **“You signed it.”**

### 16:34
Cole leaves.

Julian returns to the gala.

### 16:55
Opal begins the security disruption.

Cole expected the degraded-visibility window.

### 16:57–16:58
Rita moves into position and performs the swap.

### 16:59
Cole is photographed in the Board Lounge with:

- Hugh Stake;
- Penny Sterling.

This is a genuine alibi for the **physical swap**.

### 17:00 onward
Cole behaves normally.

He expects the plan to proceed.

### 17:12
Julian uses the counterfeit Neraval.

Cole is not present.

### ~17:28
Julian dies.

### 17:35
Death is announced.

Cole now presents himself as governance/risk leadership during the crisis he created.

## POWER — AUDIT REVIEW

Once per game, Cole chooses one currently available item tagged:

- `CORPORATE`, or
- `FINANCIAL`.

The GM gives him the prepared **control / approval history**.

This may include:

- approval chain;
- exceptions;
- control ownership;
- who signed;
- where review failed or was bypassed.

It may NOT:

- unlock unreleased evidence;
- identify the murderer directly;
- create a new approval record;
- reveal information beyond the prepared result.

The ability itself is non-transferable.

The result may be sold/traded.

## OBJECTIVES — GM INTERPRETATION

### 1. Hide architect identity
Strong success:
- fewer players correctly identify Cole as mastermind in final submissions.

Partial:
- players suspect him but cannot reconstruct planning/coordination.

### 2. Protect Rita
Strong success:
- Rita is not correctly identified as knowing accomplice / physical swapper.

Cole may abandon Rita if necessary.

The objective is not a magical loyalty requirement.

### 3. Secure governance limits
Cole should obtain meaningful commitments from the incoming CEO.

Examples:

- independent audit authority;
- limits on unilateral CEO action;
- mandatory board approval thresholds;
- transparent control exceptions;
- stronger whistleblowing / risk escalation.

At least one should come from the eventual winner for a strong success.
