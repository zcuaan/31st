# GM FILE — SALLY SHORT
## Activist Short-Seller & Investigator

**Player-facing file:** `characters/04-sally-short.md`  
**Core function:** scene tamperer / USB thief / false body-discovery point  
**Murder involvement:** NONE

---

## CORE TRUTH

Sally Short did not kill Julian Vane.

She did something that makes her look extremely guilty:

- entered Julian’s private office before the official body discovery;
- found Julian collapsed and unresponsive;
- assumed he was dead;
- stole a USB;
- disturbed the scene;
- left without telling anyone.

Crucially:

> **Julian was still alive after Sally left.**

This is later established through:

- Sarnie’s observation of movement/noise after Sally’s exit;
- wearable / office-system activity;
- refined death window evidence.

Sally is guilty of theft, concealment and scene disturbance.

She is not the fatal killer.

---

## THEMATIC FUNCTION

Sally is the game’s second major false solution.

The intended player theory is:

> Sally entered the office, found Julian, stole evidence and may have killed him or altered the scene.

That theory should feel increasingly plausible during Act II.

Then the second reversal establishes:

> Julian was alive after Sally left.

This teaches another key distinction:

- being at the scene is not the same as causing the death;
- lying about presence is not proof of homicide;
- scene tampering can distort evidence without being the fatal act.

---

## EXACT CANONICAL TIMELINE — SALLY

### Before 17:17

Sally is already investigating Vantage.

She believes the company’s public story does not match internal reality.

She is looking for documents or files that can prove it.

She has no knowledge of:

- Cole/Rita’s murder plan;
- Polly’s Aster-9 attempt;
- Opal’s blackout crime.

### 17:17

Sally secretly enters Julian’s office/private area.

She has no legitimate access.

### 17:18

Sally finds Julian:

- collapsed;
- unresponsive;
- apparently dead.

She genuinely assumes he is dead.

The GM should not treat this as a lie.

### 17:20

Sally notices and steals Julian’s USB.

While doing so, she disturbs the scene.

She then leaves.

### 17:23

After Sally has left, Sarnie hears unmistakable movement/noise from the office.

### 17:25

A wearable / office system independently records activity.

These later events prove that Julian was still alive after Sally’s departure.

### ~17:28

Julian actually dies.

### 17:34

Perry Spin and Rita Distribution enter and discover Julian dead.

### 17:35

Death is announced.

Sally now has a serious problem:

she was in the office first and stole evidence.

## THE USB

The USB is a portable game item.

It contains material connected to genuine Vantage wrongdoing.

Its exact contents should align with the master evidence registry and may include:

- financial irregularity material;
- reporting / approval records;
- material Julian had collected;
- evidence that is useful but not uniquely required for solving the murder.

Important:

The USB must not contain a single irreplaceable murder answer.

If Sally hides or loses it, the main murder remains solvable through other routes.

The USB also creates personal risk because possession helps prove Sally was at the scene.

## POWER — LEAK TO MARKET

Once per game, Sally chooses one **portable/private evidence item currently in her possession**.

She gives the ability card to the GM.

The GM makes that item’s identity / evidential existence officially public or registered.

Important:

- Sally must actually possess the item.
- The item must already be in play.
- The power does not create new evidence.
- The power does not alter the item’s contents.
- The public release cannot be reversed.
- Physical possession may remain with Sally unless the item’s specific rules say otherwise.

This power is intentionally strong because Sally’s role revolves around controlling when private information becomes public.

The ability itself is non-transferable.

The released information may then be used by everyone.

## OBJECTIVES — GM INTERPRETATION

### 1. Keep the office entry / USB theft hidden

Strong success:
- Sally’s presence in Julian’s office and theft of the USB are not broadly/finally established.

Partial success:
- players suspect she entered, but cannot prove the theft.

Failure:
- the room correctly reconstructs her office entry and USB theft.

This objective does not require her to hide all Vantage wrongdoing.

### 2. Expose genuine Vantage wrongdoing without exposing herself

Sally should try to get real wrongdoing into the investigation/public record through:

- USB material;
- Reese;
- Audie;
- other corroborating evidence;
- selective leaks.

The best outcome is:

> the wrongdoing becomes credible without the room proving Sally stole the source material.

### 3. Work out what happened after she left

Sally succeeds if she understands that:

- Julian was still alive after she left;
- later events caused the actual death.

She does not need to solve the complete Cole/Rita mechanism for this objective, although she still submits the universal final accusation.

---

## INTENDED SUSPICION ARC

### Act I — LOW TO MODERATE

Sally should look like an obvious hostile outsider / investigator.

She has motive to embarrass Vantage but no known office access yet.

### Act II — HIGH

Once office entry / witness / scene disturbance evidence appears, Sally should become a major suspect.

Players may reasonably think:

- she killed Julian;
- she stole the USB after killing him;
- she staged or disturbed the scene.

Do not weaken this too early.

### ~19:15 — SECOND REVERSAL

Release enough of:

- `WIT-004`
- `SYS-001`
- `SYS-002`
- `FOR-005`

to establish:

> Julian was alive after Sally left.

This should sharply reduce homicide suspicion without clearing her theft/tampering.

### Act III

Sally remains relevant because:

- the USB contains real wrongdoing;
- she may have hidden/traded/leaked evidence;
- scene disturbance complicates interpretation;
- she can become a useful source once she understands she did not cause the death.

---

## PLAYSTYLE GUIDANCE FOR GM

Sally’s key emotional/strategic uncertainty is:

> **“Was he actually dead when I found him?”**

Do not answer early.

If Sally asks:

> “Did I kill him?”

The canonical answer is no, but do not reveal that before the game’s timeline evidence establishes it.

If Sally says:

> “I found him already dead.”

That is her honest belief, not canonically established fact.

The GM should distinguish:

- **what Sally believed**
from
- **what actually happened**.

If the player cannot attend:

The following must survive:

- office entry around 17:17;
- USB theft;

Reassignment is preferable because the role is socially fun and mechanically useful.

## FINAL REVEAL FUNCTION

Sally should be revealed before the Neraval solution.

Recommended sequence:

1. Sally secretly entered Julian’s office.
2. She found him collapsed/unresponsive and assumed he was dead.
3. She stole the USB and disturbed the scene.
4. Sarnie / system evidence proves Julian was still alive afterwards.
5. Therefore Sally’s actions explain scene confusion but not the death.
6. The later Neraval evidence identifies the real fatal mechanism.

Recommended framing:

> Sally found a dying man, assumed she had found a dead one, and chose that moment to steal evidence.

That is bad.

It is not murder.
