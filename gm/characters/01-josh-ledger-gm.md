# GM FILE — JOSH LEDGER
## Group Strategy Director, Vantage House Group

**Player-facing file:** `characters/01-josh-ledger.md`  
**GM-only:** YES  
**Core function:** major financial red herring + succession protagonist  
**Murder involvement:** NONE

---

## CORE TRUTH

Josh is deliberately one of the strongest suspects in the game.

He is:

- ambitious
- financially compromised
- self-serving
- genuinely culpable in Vantage's reporting culture
- strongly motivated by Julian's death
- a serious Interim CEO contender

But he is **not involved in Julian Vane's murder**.

Josh did **not**:

- plan Julian's death
- assist Tom or Helen
- source or handle the counterfeit Neraval
- swap Julian's device
- put Aster-9 in Julian's champagne
- create the blackout
- enter Julian's office during the fatal sequence
- steal Julian's USB
- disturb the crime scene

Do not soften Josh's financial wrongdoing simply because he is innocent of homicide.

The intended distinction is:

> **Josh is guilty of serious financial/corporate wrongdoing. He is innocent of Julian's murder.**

## EXACT FINANCIAL WRONGDOING

Josh's wrongdoing is serious and deliberate enough to make him morally and professionally culpable.

He:

- pushed aggressive valuation and reporting decisions
- pressured colleagues to make assumptions and interpretations work in Vantage's favour
- pressed for weaker controls / control exceptions when governance slowed growth
- knew that Helix was not receiving a completely clean picture
- wanted Helix to proceed despite those concerns
- benefited personally from continued growth, valuation and succession prospects
- knew some subordinates and control functions were carrying risks that ultimately benefited him
- began trying to discover what evidence Julian had collected and who had seen it

Josh's internal rationalisation:

- the underlying business is real
- the discrepancies are temporary / manageable
- Vantage cannot grow if every risk function gets a veto
- problems can be corrected after the transaction
- senior people are paid to make difficult decisions

He is not surprised that something is wrong.

He is surprised by how far the problem has gone and by Julian's decision to stop/suspend Helix.

## THE CONTROL EXCEPTION — EXACT TRUTH

Josh pushed for a controversial control exception connected to Vantage's reporting / valuation process.

**Josh requested/pushed it.**

**Tom Balance approved it.**

The original signed document starts in **Tom's possession**.

Josh knows:

- the exception exists
- he pushed for it
- Tom signed/approved it

Josh does **not** start holding the signed document.

### Interpretation

The document is damaging to both Josh and Tom, but for different reasons.

**For Josh:**
- it supports the case that he pressured governance to weaken controls

**For Tom:**
- it shows he approved the exception
- this creates motive/conflict with Julian
- it does NOT by itself prove Tom knowingly approved fraud
- it does NOT by itself prove murder

Later, an archived corporate-record version becomes available so Tom cannot permanently erase the issue by hiding the original.

## COMMON GM QUESTIONS + ANSWERS

### "Did Josh kill Julian?"

Do not answer before the final reveal.

Use:

> "That is for you to determine from the evidence."

---

### "Was Josh really on a phone call?"

If supported by the relevant investigation/result:

> "Yes. A genuine business call occurred during the recorded period."

Do not add more than the prepared record establishes.

---

### "Does the call prove Josh could not have killed Julian?"

> "The record confirms a call. It does not account for every second of his physical movements."

---

### "Did Josh request the control exception?"

If the evidence has been obtained:

> "Yes. The document shows Josh requested/pushed the exception."

---

### "Did Tom approve it?"

If the evidence has been obtained:

> "Yes. Tom approved/signed the exception."

Do not imply that this alone proves knowing fraud or murder.

---

### "What did Julian mean by 'You signed it'?"

Before players have enough context:

> "Josh heard those words. He did not hear enough of the conversation to know exactly what Julian meant."

Do not interpret the line for them.

---

### "Did Josh know the numbers were false?"

Use the prepared nuance:

> "Josh knew some reporting and assumptions were becoming increasingly difficult to defend and still pushed for the transaction to proceed."

Avoid reducing this to either:
- "Josh knew nothing", or
- "Josh personally fabricated every number."

Neither is canon.

---

### "Did Josh know about the counterfeit Neraval?"

> "There is no established evidence that Josh knew about or handled the counterfeit Neraval."

If asked before that evidence family is in play, avoid introducing the term unnecessarily.

---

### "Can Josh steal or hide evidence?"

Yes, if it is eligible portable game evidence and normal theft rules are followed.

He cannot alter, forge or destroy official evidence.

---

### "Can Josh use Executive Authority on [future clue]?"

Only if:
- the clue is currently available
- it carries the `CORPORATE` tag
- a prepared Executive Authority result exists

Otherwise no.

### "Can Josh buy votes?"

Yes.

### "Does the attempted stabbing remove Josh from the CEO race?"
